Baku, Azerbaijan — Complete City Profile (2026)

1. City Population

Approximately 2.35–2.39 million in the city/metro area as of 2026 (estimate). Baku's metropolitan area population is estimated at 2,389,227, comprising about 22.85% of Azerbaijan's total population. This is a demographic estimate derived from UN/State Statistical Committee data, not a fresh census figure — treat as a current estimate, not an exact count. World Population Review

2. Nickname

"City of Winds" (Bādkube — Persian for "wind-pounded city"), reflecting the strong northern (Khazri) and southern (Gilavar) winds that regularly sweep the city.

3. Overview

Baku is the capital and largest city of Azerbaijan, the largest city on the Caspian Sea, and the largest city in the Caucasus region. It combines the medieval walled Old City with a modern skyline of oil-wealth architecture (Flame Towers, Heydar Aliyev Center), and functions as Azerbaijan's political, economic, scientific, and cultural capital.

4. History

Baku's roots trace to ancient settlement, with some sources citing Bronze/Iron Age evidence on the Absheron Peninsula. The first reliable written mentions of Baku appear in 5th-century sources, when the Byzantine historian Priscus of Panium described a "flame bursting from the sea rock" near the city. Arab geographers from the 9th–10th centuries described it as a small but important port city within the Shirvanshah state. After a devastating 1191 earthquake in Shamakhi, Shirvanshah Akhsitan I moved the state capital to Baku, sparking its growth; the 12th–15th centuries saw construction of the Shirvanshahs' Palace complex within the Icherisheher fortress. In 1501 the city became part of the Safavid state, and in 1806 Baku was incorporated into the Russian Empire, beginning its modern industrial era. The 19th-century oil boom transformed Baku into a major global petroleum center, and it later became capital of the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic (1918) and then the Azerbaijan SSR, before becoming capital of independent Azerbaijan in 1991. 5. Founded

Not available as an exact founding date — historians agree the precise founding date is unknown. Archaeological materials on the Absheron peninsula date to the 3rd–1st centuries BC, but the exact date on which Baku was founded is not known exactly. The city is first reliably attested in 5th-century written sources; some Azerbaijani government sources place continuous settlement in the area at roughly 5,500 years. Preslib

6. Historical Significance

Baku was capital of the Shirvanshah state (from the 12th century) and later the short-lived Baku Khanate; it became a global center of the oil industry from the 1870s onward (birthplace of the modern oil industry, home to the world's first industrially drilled oil wells) and briefly capital of the independent Azerbaijan Democratic Republic (1918–1920), the first secular democratic republic in the Muslim world. Its Old City (Icherisheher) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

7. Area

Approximately 2,130–2,200 km² (city figures vary by source; roughly 830 sq mi). Official Azerbaijani government sources state Baku covers an area of 2,200 square km. Other sources cite 2,130 km². This is an approximate/administrative figure, not a single fixed official number across all sources. Azerbaijan.az

8. Elevation

Approximately 28 meters (92 feet) below sea level — making Baku the lowest-lying national capital in the world and the largest city in the world located below sea level. This is a well-documented estimate; elevation varies slightly across the city's terrain. Latitude.to

9. Rivers

Baku has no major river running through it. The city sits on the Caspian Sea coast (Bay of Baku) and is instead characterized by salt lakes (e.g., Boyukshor, Khodasan, Bulbula) and mud volcanoes on the surrounding Absheron Peninsula rather than a river system.

10. Climate

Semi-arid/Mediterranean-influenced climate (Köppen: Csa/BSk borderline) — hot, dry summers and mild, occasionally wet winters, with the port remaining ice-free year-round. Baku sits at roughly the same latitude as Greece and Italy and generally has a Mediterranean-type climate, though it is strongly shaped by frequent strong winds (the Khazri from the north and Gilavar from the south). 12. UNESCO Sites

Walled City of Baku with the Shirvanshah's Palace and Maiden Tower (Icherisheher) — inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000. Built on a site inhabited since the Palaeolithic period, it reveals evidence of Zoroastrian, Sasanian, Arabic, Persian, Shirvani, Ottoman, and Russian presence in cultural continuity, and includes the 12th-century Maiden Tower (built over structures dating to the 7th–6th centuries BC) and the 15th-century Shirvanshahs' Palace. The site was placed on the List of World Heritage in Danger in 2003 and was removed from that endangered list in 2009 following conservation efforts. UNESCOUNESCO

13. Famous Landmarks

Maiden Tower (Qız Qalası); Shirvanshahs' Palace; Icherisheher (Old City walls); Flame Towers; Heydar Aliyev Center (designed by Zaha Hadid); Baku Boulevard (seaside promenade); Baku Ateshgah (Fire Temple, on city outskirts); Yanar Dagh (Burning Mountain); Baku City Circuit (F1 street track running through the city center).

14. Museums

Azerbaijan Carpet Museum (rug-shaped building on the Baku waterfront); Shirvanshahs' Palace Museum complex; Heydar Aliyev Center (cultural center/museum on Aliyev's life and Azerbaijani design); National Museum of History of Azerbaijan; Museum of Miniature Books; Mirza Fatali Akhundov National Library.

15. Parks

Baku Boulevard (National Seaside Park along the Caspian shore); Highland Park (Nagorny Park); Heydar Aliyev Park; Fountains Square area.

16. Traditional Food

Plov (Azerbaijani saffron rice pilaf, often with dried fruits, sometimes baked in a crispy dough crust called kazmag); dolma (grape leaves stuffed with minced meat, rice and herbs, served with garlic yogurt sauce); lyulya kebab (minced-meat skewers); dushbara (small dumpling soup); baliq (grilled Caspian fish, often sturgeon); piti (lamb stew); qovurma (lamb stewed with onions and pomegranate).

17. Street Food

Qutab (thin stuffed flatbread with meat, cheese, or greens); doner kebab; tandir bread; chestnuts and roasted corn from street vendors; fresh pomegranate and cherry juice.

18. Popular Restaurants

Firuza; Mugham Club (caravanserai-style, traditional music); Şirvanşah Muzeyi Restaurant; Dolma Restaurant; Stolovaya No 8; White City Restaurant & Bar; Sahil-area seafront dining. (Restaurant landscapes change frequently — verify current operating status directly before travel.)

Baku Jazz Festival; Novruz Bayram (Persian/Azerbaijani New Year, celebrated citywide in March with bonfires and festivities, a major national holiday). Gpdestinations

20. Cultural Events

Baku Energy Week (annual international energy conference, hosted mid-2026); 2026 Annual Meetings of the Islamic Development Bank Group (held in Baku, opened June 18, 2026); various international summits and conferences reflecting Baku's role as a regional diplomatic hub (following its hosting of COP29 in 2024 and the World Urban Forum).

21. Arts

Baku is home to a strong visual arts and design scene tied to institutions like the Heydar Aliyev Center and Azerbaijan Carpet Museum; traditional Azerbaijani carpet-weaving (itself UNESCO-recognized as Intangible Cultural Heritage) is a major artistic tradition centered in the city.

22. Music

Mugham (traditional Azerbaijani modal music, UNESCO-recognized Intangible Cultural Heritage) is closely associated with Baku, including venues like the Mugham Club. Baku hosted the Eurovision Song Contest in 2012 at Baku Crystal Hall, and has a classical music tradition tied to figures like cellist Mstislav Rostropovich and composer Uzeyir Hajibeyov, born/associated with the city.

23. Major Industries

Oil and natural gas extraction and refining (historically the core of Baku's economy, centered on the state oil company SOCAR); petrochemicals; finance and banking; construction; growing services, tourism, and technology sectors as part of Azerbaijan's economic diversification push.

24. Technology

Baku is Azerbaijan's technology and innovation hub, home to growing IT and startup activity, government "smart city" and e-government initiatives, and hosts internationally oriented conferences (energy tech, digital economy forums) as part of national diversification efforts away from oil dependency.

25. Tourism

A major and growing sector, driven by Old City heritage tourism, the Formula 1 Grand Prix, modern architecture (Flame Towers, Heydar Aliyev Center), Caspian seafront attractions, and international conference/MICE tourism. Baku's tourism board and Heydar Aliyev International Airport report continued growth in international visitor arrivals in recent years.

26. Manufacturing

Petrochemical and refined petroleum products; construction materials; light industry (textiles); food processing. Manufacturing remains closely tied to the oil and gas value chain.

27. Finance

Baku is Azerbaijan's financial capital, home to the Central Bank of Azerbaijan, the Baku Stock Exchange, and the headquarters of the country's major commercial banks and SOCAR.

28. Metro

Baku Metro — the city's rapid transit system, operated since 1967, currently comprising multiple lines (Red and Green lines being core lines referenced near the city center and F1 circuit, e.g., Icherisheher, 28 May, and Sahil stations near the Baku City Circuit). Governed by the Baku Metro authority (metro.gov.az). Platinumlist

29. Airport

Heydar Aliyev International Airport (GYD/UBBB) — located in Khazar district, Baku, about 20 km northeast of the city center, government-owned and operated by Azerbaijan Airlines, serving as the hub for Azerbaijan Airlines, Azal Avia Cargo, Silk Way West Airlines, and other carriers. It handled roughly 7,537,000 passengers in 2024, a 31.0% year-on-year increase. It is the busiest airport in the South Caucasus. WikipediaWikipedia

30. Railway

Baku Railway Station (Central Terminal), operated by Azerbaijan Railways (ADY). Located in the Nesimi district, about 3 km from the historic Old City, the station opened in 1880 and was rebuilt in 1926, and serves as the terminus for the Baku suburban railway, inter-city, and international rail lines, connected directly to the 28 May Metro station. Wikipedia

31. Bus Network

Operated under the Baku Transport Agency (BakuBus / alo141.az), covering city and suburban routes; integrated with the BakuCard fare payment system shared with the Metro.

32. Major Roads

35. Sports

Football (most popular sport; home to top Azerbaijani Premier League clubs); Formula 1 motor racing (Baku City Circuit, hosting the Azerbaijan Grand Prix since 2016); chess (Baku has a strong chess culture, producing champions like Garry Kasparov and Teimour Radjabov); Baku hosted the 2015 European Games and 2017 Islamic Solidarity Games.

36. Stadiums

Baku Olympic Stadium (Tofiq Bahramov stadium's successor as national stadium, hosted UEFA Europa League Final 2019 and Euro 2020 matches); Tofiq Bahramov Republican Stadium (historic national stadium, named after the Azerbaijani linesman from the 1966 World Cup Final); Baku City Circuit (F1 street circuit, not a fixed stadium but a purpose-built race track through the city).

37. Famous People

Garry Kasparov — World Chess Champion (1985–2000), born in Baku in 1963, widely regarded as one of the greatest chess players in history. Wikipedia

Lev Landau — Nobel Prize-winning Soviet physicist, born in Baku, known for foundational work in quantum mechanics.

Mstislav Rostropovich — renowned Soviet/Russian cellist and conductor, born in Baku in 1927. Wikipedia

Ilham Aliyev — current President of Azerbaijan, born in Baku (1961).

Heydar Aliyev — former President of Azerbaijan (Soviet-era leader and post-independence president), strongly associated with the city that now hosts the Heydar Aliyev Center and Heydar Aliyev International Airport in his name.

Teimour Radjabov — chess grandmaster, became one of the youngest-ever International Grandmasters.

Uzeyir Hajibeyov — composer, founder of modern Azerbaijani classical/mugham opera, closely associated with Baku.

Lotfi A. Zadeh — mathematician and computer scientist, founder of fuzzy logic, born in Baku.

39. World Records

Baku holds the distinction of being the world's lowest-lying national capital city and the largest city in the world located below sea level (approx. 28 m/92 ft below sea level).

40. Interesting Facts

Baku's name is linked etymologically to Persian terms associated with wind ("Bādkube," roughly "wind-pounded city"), reflecting its famously strong seasonal winds (Khazri and Gilavar); the surrounding Absheron Peninsula features active mud volcanoes, a geological rarity concentrated in this region.

41. Unique Customs

Novruz Bayram (Persian New Year, March 20–21) is celebrated with citywide bonfires, street festivities, and traditional foods, marking one of the most important cultural events in Baku and across Azerbaijan; hospitality customs around tea-drinking in traditional chaikhanas (teahouses) remain a strong social tradition.

42. Unique Transportation

The Baku Funicular, connecting the city center to Highland Park (Nagorny Park) on the upper terraces overlooking the Caspian Sea, offers a distinctive scenic transit option alongside the Metro and bus network.

43. Surprising Facts

Despite being a coastal Caspian Sea city, Baku sits below sea level rather than at or above it — a highly unusual geographic feature for a major capital; the surrounding region's mud volcanoes are among the largest concentrations of such formations anywhere on Earth.
