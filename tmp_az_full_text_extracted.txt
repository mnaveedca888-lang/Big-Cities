2. Nickname
"City of Winds" (Bādkube — Persian for "wind-pounded city"), reflecting the strong northern (Khazri) and southern (Gilavar) winds that regularly sweep the city.
3. Overview
Baku is the capital and largest city of Azerbaijan, the largest city on the Caspian Sea, and the largest city in the Caucasus region. It combines the medieval walled Old City with a modern skyline of oil-wealth architecture (Flame Towers, Heydar Aliyev Center), and functions as Azerbaijan's political, economic, scientific, and cultural capital.

6. Historical Significance
Baku was capital of the Shirvanshah state (from the 12th century) and later the short-lived Baku Khanate; it became a global center of the oil industry from the 1870s onward (birthplace of the modern oil industry, home to the world's first industrially drilled oil wells) and briefly capital of the independent Azerbaijan Democratic Republic (1918–1920), the first secular democratic republic in the Muslim world. Its Old City (Icherisheher) is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

21. Arts
Baku is home to a strong visual arts and design scene tied to institutions like the Heydar Aliyev Center and Azerbaijan Carpet Museum; traditional Azerbaijani carpet-weaving (itself UNESCO-recognized as Intangible Cultural Heritage) is a major artistic tradition centered in the city.
22. Music
Mugham (traditional Azerbaijani modal music, UNESCO-recognized Intangible Cultural Heritage) is closely associated with Baku, including venues like the Mugham Club. Baku hosted the Eurovision Song Contest in 2012 at Baku Crystal Hall, and has a classical music tradition tied to figures like cellist Mstislav Rostropovich and composer Uzeyir Hajibeyov, born/associated with the city.
23. Major Industries
Oil and natural gas extraction and refining (historically the core of Baku's economy, centered on the state oil company SOCAR); petrochemicals; finance and banking; construction; growing services, tourism, and technology sectors as part of Azerbaijan's economic diversification push.
24. Technology
Baku is Azerbaijan's technology and innovation hub, home to growing IT and startup activity, government "smart city" and e-government initiatives, and hosts internationally oriented conferences (energy tech, digital economy forums) as part of national diversification efforts away from oil dependency.
25. Tourism
international conference/MICE tourism. Baku's tourism board and Heydar Aliyev International Airport report continued growth in international visitor arrivals in recent years.
26. Manufacturing
Petrochemical and refined petroleum products; construction materials; light industry (textiles); food processing. Manufacturing remains closely tied to the oil and gas value chain.
27. Finance
Baku is Azerbaijan's financial capital, home to the Central Bank of Azerbaijan, the Baku Stock Exchange, and the headquarters of the country's major commercial banks and SOCAR.

40. Interesting Facts
Baku's name is linked etymologically to Persian terms associated with wind ("Bādkube," roughly "wind-pounded city"), reflecting its famously strong seasonal winds (Khazri and Gilavar); the surrounding Absheron Peninsula features active mud volcanoes, a geological rarity concentrated in this region.
41. Unique Customs
Novruz Bayram (Persian New Year, March 20–21) is celebrated with citywide bonfires, street festivities, and traditional foods, marking one of the most important cultural events in Baku and across Azerbaijan; hospitality customs around tea-drinking in traditional chaikhanas (teahouses) remain a strong social tradition.
42. Unique Transportation
The Baku Funicular, connecting the city center to Highland Park (Nagorny Park) on the upper terraces overlooking the Caspian Sea, offers a distinctive scenic transit option alongside the Metro and bus network.
43. Surprising Facts
Despite being a coastal Caspian Sea city, Baku sits below sea level rather than at or above it — a highly unusual geographic feature for a major capital; the surrounding region's mud volcanoes are among the largest concentrations of such formations anywhere on Earth.

3. Overview
Fuzuli is a city in southwestern Azerbaijan, capital of Fuzuli District, located in the Karabakh economic region near the foothills of the Lesser Caucasus. Named after the celebrated 16th-century Azerbaijani/Ottoman poet Muhammad Fuzuli, the city was occupied by Armenian forces from 1993 to 2020 and left largely in ruins. Since its recapture by Azerbaijan in October 2020, Fuzuli has become a centerpiece of the government's Karabakh reconstruction program, including a new international airport and planned "smart city" infrastructure.

3. Overview
Lankaran is a city in southeastern Azerbaijan on the Caspian Sea coast, near the southern border with Iran, and serves as the administrative center of the Lankaran-Astara Economic Region. Backed by the Talysh Mountains, it is known for its humid subtropical climate, tea and citrus cultivation, distinct Talysh cultural heritage, and status as former capital of the historic Talysh Khanate.
